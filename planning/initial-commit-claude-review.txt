MEDIUM
M1 The stable module GraphQL/Response.lean depends on an experimental module.
   - It imports GraphQL.Plan.WireScalar, and its stable `jsonString` (and therefore
     `ResponseValue.encode`, `encodeCompletion` and `requestError`) is defined as
     `Plan.WireScalar.quote`.
   - Under the current wording, WireScalar is experimental. A change to
     WireScalar.quote/escapeChar would change the observable output of stable functions,
     and the policy does not say whether that counts as a "documented semantics" change.
   - Every `import GraphQL.Response` also brings GraphQL.Plan.WireScalar.* into scope.
     Downstream users may come to rely on those names without any stability guarantee.
     Your re-export sentence covers this only for `import GraphQL`.
   Recommended, before 1.0 (not before this commit), one of:
   (a) state that the behavior of stable declarations is stable whatever modules implement
       it, and that transitive imports of stable modules are not themselves stable; or
   (b) promote WireScalar.quote, its Quoted/Escaped relations and quote_correct to the
       stable list; or
   (c) give Response.lean its own definitions.
   Option (a) is the smallest edit.

LOW
L1 Typeclass instances are not mentioned.
   - Stable types derive Repr, BEq, DecidableEq and Inhabited, and Schema.lean also declares
     two Decidable instances and Machine.lean a Monad instance.
   - Downstream code depends on these instances existing, but the policy lists only
     constructors, fields, function types and theorems. Their instance names are
     compiler-generated and therefore excluded.
   - Suggest: "the availability of their instances (names excluded)". Also mention default
     field values, such as Schema.query := "Query" and Completion.value's errors := [].
L2 Guarantee preservation in minor releases is ambiguous.
   - "Each release must preserve this chain or explicitly narrow its claims before
     publication; a loss of a documented guarantee is not a compatible 1.x change." This can
     be read as allowing a 1.x minor release to narrow claims, provided it does so
     explicitly.
   - Say directly whether narrowing a reference-application guarantee requires 2.0. Also say
     how that interacts with the reference-application APIs being experimental: the
     guarantees are stable, but their Lean names are not.
L3 Small undefined terms.
   - "compatible upgrades" of the toolchain: suggest "upgrades under which code within the
     compatibility promise still elaborates".
   - The release gate's "minimal Lake consumer" will need the package name `leanGraphql` and
     the git/rev form of `require`. That is worth one line in the policy or release notes.
L4 Local paths in committed review reports.
   - About 33 planning/*claude*.txt files end with "REVIEW COMPLETE /Users/hareisu/...".
     Several, including my own lean-harness, s6b and s6cd reports, cite /private/tmp log
     paths. planning/plan-draft.txt:3 records the workspace path.
   - This exposes a macOS account name (`hareisu`, different from the copyright name) and
     paths that do not resolve for anyone else.
   - It is not secret, and the policy says to keep reviews verbatim, so preserving them is
     defensible. If you'd rather not publish the account name, a mechanical path rewrite

     them.
L5 .gitattributes wording and line endings.
   - The comment "Exclude disposable agent material even if explicitly added to Git" is
     accurate only for `git archive`. export-ignore does not keep such files out of the
     repository or out of clones. Suggest "Exclude ... from source archives (git archive)

   - Optional: add `* text=auto eol=lf`, or at least `scripts/* text eol=lf`, so a Windows
     checkout with autocrlf cannot break the POSIX bootstrap scripts. Today every file has

L6 planning/release-2.0-draft.md (follow-up included).
